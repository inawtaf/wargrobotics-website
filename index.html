Premium video transmission

Digital from the sensor to the screen, with no analogue stage anywhere in the path. Picture quality is what separates the units in the range; everything else about them is deliberately the same.

HD digital
No analogue stage, so no static, no rolling interference and no slow fade at the edge of range. The picture is either there or it is not.
60 ms glass to glass
Measured end to end on the bench rather than summed from component datasheets.
Frame rate where it counts
720p at 90 fps on Ghost for fast flying, 1080p at 30 fps on Greywind for detail, with 720p90 available on it too.
4K recording next
BloodRaven records above what it transmits: 1080p live with 4K written to storage. Not yet announced for sale.

Record while you fly

The module records to its own storage at the same time as it streams to your screen. You are not choosing between seeing the flight and keeping it, and the recording is written on the air unit rather than reconstructed from the transmitted stream.

Live and record together
Both run at once. The link going down does not take the recording with it.
Onboard storage
8 GB on Ghost, 16 GB on Greywind. No card to buy, lose or forget.
Straight off over USB
Plug the air unit into a computer and pull the files. No proprietary reader and no app in the middle.

Ready to go

No soldering, no firmware rabbit hole, no forum thread at midnight. The module arrives bound and configured, and everything you change afterwards you change from your phone.

Plug and play
One cable to power and four bolts to mount. Nothing to flash before you get a picture.
Five minute setup
Box to first frame in about five minutes, including binding the ground unit.
Mobile and web app
Channel, bitrate, recording and firmware from your phone. Updates arrive over the air.

Fits what you already fly

Where it is flying3

Four platforms the module runs on today, with what we have proven and what we have not.

In production

FPV and freestyle

The original case, and still the hardest. Below about seventy milliseconds the link stops being something a pilot compensates for, which is the entire point.

Proven

  • 60 ms sustained through gates and tight lines
  • Link holds under hard banking and prop wash
  • Standard 20 by 20 stack, no adapter plate

Not yet

  • No published figure for multi-pilot channel density
  • Untested below -5 °C
  • No head-tracking integration
Field trials

Industrial inspection

Range and encryption matter more than latency here. Assets sit a long way from an operator, and the footage is often evidence, so the transport has to be defensible as well as clear.

Proven

  • Stable link at extended standoff2
  • Stable frame at hover under moderate wind
  • Swappable optics for close-in detail work

Not yet

  • No thermal or multispectral variant shipping
  • No chain-of-custody or tamper-evident recording
  • No BVLOS regulatory package
Field trials

Ground robotics

Same module, same mount, bolted to rovers instead of airframes. Ground platforms trade line of sight for vibration and obstruction, which is a different problem from the air but not a harder one.

Proven

  • Runs unmodified off the vehicle's own bus
  • Survives sustained chassis vibration
  • Low mounting height, no gimbal required

Not yet

  • No figure for range with terrain obstruction
  • No sealed or IP-rated enclosure
  • No mesh or relay between vehicles
Pilot programme

Research platforms

Groups building control systems need a video link whose latency is repeatable, not merely low. A number that drifts is worse than a number that is high, because you can design around a high one.

Proven

  • Latency holds within a tight band run to run
  • Deterministic behaviour at fixed bitrate
  • Bench-repeatable against a calibration target

Not yet

  • No published jitter distribution
  • No open SDK or timestamp API
  • No ROS integration

Built around your constraint

For a manufacturer or an integrator, the module is specified to your vehicle instead of the other way round.

Imaging to your optical path

  • Lens and field of view selected per airframe
  • Sensor swapped where the light demands it
  • Global shutter for measurement work

Transport tuned to your environment

  • Channel and frequency plan per deployment
  • Bitrate matched to the link budget
  • Range specified to your regulatory ceiling2

Chassis cut to your vehicle

  • Mount pattern and stack height to fit
  • Connector orientation and cable exit
  • Weight budget agreed before tooling

An open feed you can build on

  • Standard output into your own pipeline
  • Feeds image-processing and detection layers
  • No proprietary decoder in the path
